## system

Wolfram Language / Mathematica (Wolfram Research), including the Entity framework curated-data layer, the Quantity units framework, and Wolfram|Alpha

## overlap

Wolfram Language is the closest shipped instance of "one typed symbolic system across mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology." Mechanisms that overlap the proposed product: (1) A symbolic knowledge layer — Entity["Chemical",…], EntityValue, EntityClass over hundreds of curated domains (Chemical, Species, Planet, City, Company, Person…), backed by the same Wolfram Knowledgebase that powers Wolfram|Alpha, with EntityStore/EntityRegister for user-defined typed vocabularies (reference.wolfram.com KnowledgeRepresentationAndAccess guide, accessed 2026-08-26). (2) A units framework — Quantity[mag, unit] covering the NIST SP 811 unit set plus physical constants, CompatibleUnitQ dimensional checking, UnitConvert, and a deliberate type split between absolute temperatures and temperature differences, i.e., typed unit semantics of the kind the product wants (Quantity ref page, accessed 2026-08-26). (3) An industrial symbolic-mathematics engine (Integrate, Reduce, DSolve, quantifier elimination) fused with numerics (NDSolve) in one evaluator — the "symbolic + numerical under one IR" ambition, though solvers are in-process and proprietary, not orchestrated external typed backends. (4) Proof objects exist in one niche: FindEquationalProof produces a ProofObject with ProofDataset (every step), ProofGraph, and an executable ProofFunction, over equational/first-order axiom systems via Knuth–Bendix completion (ref page, accessed 2026-08-26) — the proof-carrying-inference idea, but confined to user-supplied axiom systems, not to the main solver stack. (5) Temporal qualifiers: EntityValue supports Dated[...] property values — a partial analogue of evidence-with-time. (6) Wolfram|Alpha adds free-form natural-language ingress over the same curated data — the opposite pole from the product's fail-closed KR surface, but the same "state a scientific question, get a computed answer" goal. The overlap is therefore at the level of goals and of two concrete mechanisms worth copying (entity-typed curated vocabulary, dimensioned quantities in the evaluator), not at the level of auditability, provenance, determinism, or licensing, where the two designs are near-inverses.

## insufficiency

Specific missing mechanisms, each verified against Wolfram's own documentation: (1) No proof objects for the main engine. Integrate/Reduce/DSolve/NDSolve return values, not certificates; the official "Some Notes on Internal Implementation" page states the notes "give only a rough indication of basic methods and algorithms used" and that e.g. Reduce is ~350 pages of WL plus ~1400 pages of closed C — so a result cannot be audited against a disclosed algorithm, and the license prohibits decompiling/reverse engineering, closing the other route. (2) No provenance receipts. The Wolfram|Alpha FAQ concedes that "most of the data in Wolfram|Alpha is derived by computations, often based on multiple sources," making individual data points untraceable; there is a page-level "Sources" button, not per-claim citation, hashes, or solver identities. (3) No replay mechanism. The vendor's own citation guidance is to cite "Wolfram|Alpha" plus "the time and/or place at which the query was made, as it can affect the result" — time-of-query as a citation field is an admission that results are not reproducible. EntityValue documents caching and background fetching but no Knowledgebase version pinning or snapshot addressing. (4) No fail-closed surface: Quantity "will automatically attempt to parse an unknown unit string" heuristically — silent defaulting where the product demands a compile error; W|A's linguistic guessing is the same property at sentence scale. (5) No uncertainty taxonomy: nothing distinguishes measurement vs numerical vs parameter vs structural uncertainty as typed claims (Around[] exists for value±error propagation — evidence-incomplete, not re-verified). (6) No verdict/disposition result taxonomy; failures surface as unevaluated expressions, Failure objects, or messages. (7) Not a linkable permissively licensed library: proprietary EULA, no redistribution, free engine restricted to pre-production use.

## narrowing_history

The computational core did not narrow — it has monotonically broadened from Mathematica 1.0 (1988) through Wolfram Language 15.0.1 (July 2026), absorbing ML, LLM tooling, and more data domains each release (quick-revision-history, accessed 2026-08-26). What did narrow is the knowledge-supply and distribution ambition around Wolfram|Alpha. Stephen Wolfram's own tenth-anniversary retrospective (2019, accessed 2026-08-26) records two retreats with reasons: (a) crowd-sourced/volunteer curation — "a few hundred active volunteers" circa 2010 — was abandoned except in trivia niches because "it's become much more difficult for outside talent to be effective" once tooling and judgement-call processes matured; his stated principle became "there always has to be an expert involved—or you'll never get the right answer." (b) The consumer "answer engine for everything" positioning settled into a free website plus paid APIs, ~50 specialized apps, assistant integrations, and enterprise custom-curation consulting (Wolfram Solutions) — i.e., the universal-knowledge ambition narrowed to domains where level-9/level-10 curation (his 10-step hierarchy; level 10 = complete, no-missing-members coverage required for Wolfram Language-grade data) could actually be paid for. The general lesson of the history is that ambition survived only where a funded expert pipeline existed; everywhere else coverage stayed at Wolfram|Alpha's shallower level or was dropped.

## license

Proprietary, closed source — no SPDX open-source id applies (LicenseRef-Proprietary). Verified from license texts: the Mathematica License Agreement (wolfram.com/legal/agreements/wolfram-mathematica/, accessed 2026-08-26) grants a non-exclusive use license, prohibits "distributing, publishing, transferring, sublicensing, lending, leasing, renting" and "decompiling, disassembling or reverse engineering." The Free Wolfram Engine for Developers Terms (wolfram.com/legal/terms/wolfram-engine.html, accessed 2026-08-26) allow only "pre-production/pre-distribution development," personal non-commercial projects, and Wolfram-approved open-source projects; running it "in a production software application" or "expressly to produce output for commercial or organizational use" requires a paid license; modification and redistribution are forbidden. Distribution model: paid desktop/cloud/site licenses (Mathematica, Wolfram|One), free-for-developers Wolfram Engine kernel, free Wolfram|Alpha website with paid API tiers, and bundled hosted services. Nothing in the stack can be forked, vendored, or shipped inside a permissively licensed product.

## adoption

Observable evidence: 38 years of continuous commercial releases, current version 15.0.1 (July 2026) with 15.0 (June 2026) and 14.3 (August 2025) — seen on wolfram.com's quick-revision-history, accessed 2026-08-26 — which is strong evidence of a sustained paying customer base. Vendor-stated (Wolfram's own 2019 blog, not independently verified): Wolfram|Alpha integrated into Siri and Alexa, "many billions of queries," ~50 specialized apps, API customers "large and small," on-premise deployments for the largest customers, NASA websites, tutoring systems, smart-contract oracles. I did not fetch third-party market-share, citation-count, or user-count data during this task, so all magnitude claims beyond release cadence are evidence-incomplete. Honest reading: dominant in symbolic-math education and entrenched in pockets of research/engineering, but the Entity/Knowledgebase layer's scientific adoption is thin relative to the symbolic engine's — researchers cite results but cannot audit them, and the license keeps it out of open scientific toolchains, which is precisely the gap the proposed product targets.

## lessons

Copy: (1) the Quantity discipline — a NIST-anchored unit vocabulary, dimensional compatibility as a hard check, and fine-grained semantic splits like absolute temperature vs temperature difference; this is the best-tested typed-units design in existence. (2) Entity-typed vocabulary with named properties and user-extensible EntityStores — one uniform mechanism from chemistry to geography. (3) Wolfram's curation doctrine, stated from experience: machine learning only suggests, "you need a human expert who can exercise judgement," and his 10-level hierarchy — data usable for question-answering (level 9) is categorically weaker than data usable for computation over ALL members of a class with none missing (level 10). Budget for level 10 or don't claim it. (4) ProofObject's shape (step dataset + graph + replayable function) is a good target for what a proof artifact should contain. Mistake to avoid: the trust-me black box. Wolfram couples the broadest scientific coverage ever assembled to zero auditability — algorithms disclosed only as a "rough indication," reverse engineering contractually banned, per-datum provenance admitted to be untraceable, no Knowledgebase versioning, and official citation guidance that amounts to "record when you asked." A second mistake: silent heuristic defaulting (unknown unit strings auto-parsed, free-form linguistics guessing intent) — the exact opposite of fail-closed, and the source of the silent-wrong-answer class the proposed product exists to eliminate. Third: single-vendor vocabulary governance makes the entire scientific vocabulary supply chain unforkable and mortal with the company.

## cluster_answer

What the stack actually delivers: the only shipped system where one language spans curated entities across sciences, dimensioned quantities, industrial symbolic math, and numerics — and it genuinely works interactively at that breadth. That existence proof matters: breadth is achievable inside one typed symbolic substrate. Auditability/provenance/proof gaps: a Wolfram result can generally be neither independently audited nor replayed. Audited: no — the engine is closed source, the license bans reverse engineering, and the official implementation notes state they give "only a rough indication of basic methods"; proof objects exist only for FindEquationalProof's equational niche, and even those are checked by re-running Wolfram (FullSimplify/ProofFunction), not by an independent verifier. Replayed: no — Knowledgebase data is fetched and cached from Wolfram servers with no version pinning or snapshot addressing documented, data updates silently, and Wolfram's own FAQ tells academics to cite the time of the query "as it can affect the result." Provenance: a page-level "Sources" button plus an admission that most data "is derived by computations, often based on multiple sources" and cannot be traced datum-by-datum. So every axis the proposed product makes first-class (hashes, solver identities, seeds, replay instructions, per-claim provenance) is structurally absent, not merely unshipped. Governance lesson of single-vendor curation: the cost is enormous and permanent — a decades-long, expert-staffed pipeline (140k+ foods, 350k+ notable people, 1.6B census points added in one decade; per-domain bespoke work every time), crowd-sourcing was tried and abandoned because judgement-call quality couldn't be maintained, and "there always has to be an expert involved." Under one vendor that investment is rented, not owned: unversioned, unforkable, license-locked, and it dies or drifts with the company. A new product should treat the curated vocabulary as an open, versioned, hash-pinned artifact with an explicit expert-review supply chain — and should budget curation, not the solver, as the dominant long-term cost.
