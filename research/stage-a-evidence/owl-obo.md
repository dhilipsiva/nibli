## system

The Semantic Web / OWL science-ontology programme: OBO Foundry (GO, ChEBI, Uberon), the BioPortal repository, description-logic reasoners (ELK, HermiT), and SNOMED CT as the large-terminology comparison point.

## overlap

Mechanism-level overlap with the proposed product is substantial on the KR-and-governance half and near-zero on the computation half. (1) Governed, fail-closed vocabulary: OBO Foundry principles make each term a stable IRI in a registered PURL namespace (P3), with mandatory textual definitions (P6), formal releases (P4), declared scope/orthogonality (P5), reused relations from a central Relations Ontology (P7), and term stability — a changed referent requires a new term (P19). This is exactly the proposed product's "undeclared vocabulary is a compile error" discipline, implemented socially plus tooling rather than in a compiler. (2) Deterministic compilation with verdicts: OBO release pipelines run ROBOT `reason`, which "will always perform a logical validation check prior to automatic classification" — consistency check, unsatisfiable-class detection (a class-level analog of invalid_model), then materialization of the inferred class hierarchy. ELK does this in polynomial time for OWL 2 EL and produces explanations "showing how logical consequences derive from axioms" — a proof-object analog. SNOMED CT's release process likewise uses an EL classifier to compute its inferred (distribution) relationship form from stated axioms; that classification step is literally a deterministic model compiler for a ~350k-concept terminology. (3) Typed declarative statements: OWL axioms over typed classes/properties, with the OWL 2 EL profile as a deliberately restricted tractable fragment (PTIME-complete subsumption per the W3C Recommendation). (4) Registry/hosted-service surface: BioPortal hosts ~1,283 ontologies with search, mappings, and a REST API — the "hosted service over shared models" form the product later intends. (5) Result discipline: entailed / not-entailed / inconsistent / unsatisfiable is a small ancestor of the product's verdict axis. What the programme demonstrates end-to-end: a solo-scale team can maintain a large formal artifact if identifier governance, CI validation gates, and versioned releases carry the weight.

## insufficiency

Missing mechanisms, not ambitions: (1) No quantities. OWL has no units, dimensions, reference frames, or numeric computation; datatype facets are the ceiling, and OWL 2 EL drops even cardinality, negation, disjunction, universal restriction, and inverse properties (W3C profiles spec). A DL classifier cannot evaluate an equation, propagate an uncertainty, or run a simulation — the entire "orchestrator" half of the product has no counterpart. (2) No uncertainty type system. OWL axioms are crisp; evidence enters GO only as annotation evidence codes attached outside the logic, so measurement/parameter/structural uncertainty is not representable, let alone kept distinct. (3) Open-world, monotonic, class-level semantics: no closed-world verdicts ("not derivable" is not expressible as a result), no defaults/exceptions (biological generalizations like "mammals are four-limbed" are either false axioms or omitted), weak instance (A-box) story at scale. (4) Binary properties only: n-ary scientific relations (a measurement of X in system Y under condition Z with error E) require reification patterns; there are no named argument places — the exact pain the product's predicate-call surface removes. (5) Provenance is metadata, not receipts: axiom annotations and version IRIs exist, but nothing binds a conclusion to source/IR hashes, reasoner identity+version, or replay instructions; two reasoners can disagree (and historically did) with no machine-checkable certificate — ELK's explanations are proof-shaped but not packaged as reproducibility artifacts. (6) No disposition axis: a reasoner timeout, a profile violation, and a modeling error all surface as tool-specific failures, not typed results. (7) The expressive reasoners needed for richer semantics are effectively abandoned: HermiT (full OWL 2 DL, hypertableau) sits at 1.3.8 on OWL API 3.4.3 — a decade-stale toolchain — so the only maintained lane is the EL fragment, which cannot state most scientific constraints.

## narrowing_history

It narrowed twice, with evidence. First, semantics: OWL full DL (SROIQ) reasoning proved intractable on real terminologies, and the W3C's answer was the 2009/2012 OWL 2 Profiles Recommendation — EL/QL/RL, each "trad[ing] some expressive power for the efficiency of reasoning" (W3C, 11 Dec 2012). The field then consolidated onto EL: ELK (EL-only, PTIME) is Apache-2.0, 2,336 commits, latest release 0.6.0, while HermiT (full DL) is frozen at 1.3.8 against OWL API 3.4.3 with no dated release since the early 2010s. SNOMED CT likewise fits the EL profile (per its OWL refset documentation, disjointness aside). Second, purpose: the 2007 OBO Foundry Nature Biotechnology paper (Smith et al., nbt1346) promised ontologies "interoperable and logically well formed" that "incorporate accurate representations of biological reality" to support data integration — a reasoning-forward vision. In practice the deliverables that survived are annotation vocabularies, identifier governance, and QC pipelines: ROBOT's reason command exists to validate and materialize hierarchies for release, not to answer scientific queries; GO ships a deliberately logic-weakened "go-basic" (acyclic, no has_part) as the recommended artifact because downstream tools want a safe DAG, not axioms. The wider Semantic Web ambition (autonomous agents over machine-readable knowledge) contracted to controlled vocabularies plus lookup services (BioPortal). The cause is mechanical: the decidable fragment that scales cannot express what scientists need, and the fragment that can express more does not scale and its reasoners lost maintenance.

## adoption

The vocabularies won; the reasoning mostly did not leave the release pipeline. Observable: BioPortal listed 1,283 hosted ontologies as of July 2026 (search-result snapshot; the site itself returned 403 to direct fetch). Uberon shows a professionally maintained artifact: 6,022 commits, 251 open issues, 39 open PRs, ODK/ROBOT CI (GitHub, accessed 2026-08-26). ChEBI reports 195,000+ entries (EBI, accessed 2026-08-26). GO ships three release artifacts in OBO/OWL/JSON and anchors the enrichment-analysis ecosystem (its citation dominance is well known but I did not fetch a citation count — evidence-incomplete). SNOMED CT is nationally mandated infrastructure across SNOMED International's member countries with a formal licensing bureaucracy (MLDS annual statements of usage — a signal of real deployment governance). Reasoner adoption is narrower: ELK is alive (release 0.6.0; 124 stars — small, because its users are pipelines, not people) and is ROBOT's default reasoner; HermiT is effectively unmaintained yet still cited/bundled, indicating demand for full-DL reasoning died rather than migrated. Honest summary: tens of thousands of curators and virtually every genomics database consume the identifiers and hierarchies daily; the DL semantics are exercised almost exclusively by a handful of release engineers running ROBOT.

## lessons

Copy: (1) The identifier contract — opaque stable IDs under a governed PURL namespace, mandatory human-readable definitions beside every formal term, deprecation-not-deletion with term-stability rules (P19: changed meaning = new ID). This is the single most successful piece of scientific KR infrastructure ever shipped and the product's fail-closed name resolution should bind to this style of registry, and ideally to the existing OBO/ChEBI/Uberon IDs themselves rather than minting competitors. (2) Governance as principles + dashboard: scope/orthogonality declarations, a named locus of authority, community review with response-time norms, and automated conformance checking (OBO Dashboard). (3) Reasoning as a CI gate: ROBOT's validate-then-materialize on every release — the product's "deterministic compilation with typed refusals" is this pattern promoted into the language. (4) Tractable-core discipline: ELK survived because EL's PTIME contract made classification a dependable build step; keep the always-on logical layer inside a fragment with predictable cost and push everything else to explicitly-budgeted batch obligations (which the product's latency model already does). Avoid: (1) OWL's semantics as the computational substrate — open-world, monotonic, class-level, binary-property logic forced reification contortions and could never touch quantities; the product's closed-world typed predicate calls with named places are the right divergence. (2) Reasoning as the value proposition: the payoff of DL was QC and hierarchy maintenance, and projects that promised scientific inference from axioms under-delivered for two decades; the product must locate its value in the solver-orchestration and receipt layer the ontology programme never had. (3) Letting the expressive tier depend on unmaintained hero tools (HermiT): every solver dependency needs an identity-and-version receipt and a replacement story.

## cluster_answer

The genuine success and the failure separate cleanly along "what a classifier can conclude." A DL classifier computes exactly: (a) the complete subsumption preorder over class expressions (the inferred is-a hierarchy), (b) satisfiability of each class and consistency of the whole ontology, and (c) membership of a queried class expression in that preorder (e.g., SNOMED expression subsumption: is this post-coordinated clinical expression a kind of that concept?). In OWL 2 EL this is PTIME-complete and total; that is the entire menu. Scientists genuinely needed three things off it: hierarchy maintenance at scale (SNOMED's ~350k-concept inferred form is produced by an EL classifier — hand-curating it is impossible; GO's cross-product axioms let ELK auto-place terms), error detection (unsatisfiable classes and inconsistencies caught by ROBOT before release), and subsumption-closed retrieval (query expansion over is-a for record retrieval and annotation propagation). Everything else scientists compute — enrichment statistics, kinetics, dosing, phylogenies, differential equations — is arithmetic, statistical, or causal, and subsumption cannot express it; EL cannot even say "exactly two," "not," or "or," while the full DL that says more is N2ExpTime-hard and its reasoners (HermiT, frozen at 1.3.8) lost maintenance. Tellingly, the largest computational payoff attributed to GO — enrichment analysis — uses the ontology as a plain DAG inside external statistical tools; the DL semantics contribute nothing at query time, which is why GO's recommended artifact is the logic-weakened acyclic "go-basic". So the investment bought a superb controlled-vocabulary compiler and QC gate, mistaken for a science engine. The proposed product should reuse: the OBO identifier/PURL/deprecation contract (bind to existing GO/ChEBI/Uberon IDs), the principles-plus-dashboard governance and term-review workflow, versioned multi-format releases, and the ROBOT pattern of validation-before-materialization as compiler semantics; a tractable EL-like fragment is a sound choice for its taxonomy backbone. It should avoid: OWL's open-world monotonic binary-property semantics for empirical claims, reification in place of named argument places, any promise that logical classification computes science — classification is the product's compile step, and the scientific payoff must come from the typed solver orchestration, uncertainty types, and reproducibility receipts that this programme structurally lacked.

## largest_unverified_assumption

That the computational payoff of DL reasoning is confined to release-time QC, hierarchy materialization, and subsumption retrieval — this is inferred from the mechanics of what EL classification can conclude plus ROBOT's documented pipeline role and GO's logic-weakened recommended artifact, not from a fetched systematic survey of reasoner usage; a significant scientific-inference success story using OWL semantics beyond classification could weaken the 'avoid the semantics' recommendation, though none surfaced in this research.
