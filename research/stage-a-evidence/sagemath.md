## system

SageMath (sagemath/sage) — free open-source mathematics software system founded by William Stein in 2005; mission "creating a viable free open source alternative to Magma, Maple, Mathematica and Matlab"; ~90+ upstream FOSS packages (Maxima, GAP, PARI, FLINT, Singular, NumPy/SciPy, R, …) unified behind a Python/Cython library of 700,000+ lines.

## overlap

SageMath is the closest existing instance of the "one typed surface, many delegated backends" architecture the proposed product describes, at mechanism level: (1) Its parent/element coercion framework is a working cross-domain type system for mathematics — every object carries a first-class "parent" (Integer Ring, GF(p), QQ['x'], …); binary operations resolve via registered canonical coercion morphisms, and when no direct coercion exists, a pushout algorithm over construction functors synthesizes a common parent (ZZ['x'] + QQ → QQ['x']). This is exactly the "typed declarative KR across mathematics" unification problem, solved operationally rather than declaratively. (2) It is a solver orchestrator in fact: symbolic integration/limits delegate to Maxima (and Giac/SymPy), group theory to GAP, number theory to PARI/FLINT, Gröbner bases to Singular, statistics to R — through a mixed interface layer of C-library bindings (libgap, cypari) and text/pexpect subprocess interfaces that marshal objects between semantic models. (3) Reproducibility-by-pinning: the SPKG distribution vendors exact, tested-together versions of every dependency — a crude ancestor of the proposed "solver identities and exact versions" receipts, implemented at the whole-distribution level instead of per-result. (4) A 200,000+-line doctest corpus acts as a behavioral conformance contract binding the wrapper layer to backend behavior across upgrades — the analogue of per-solver conformance gates. (5) A deterministic preparser turns user input into Sage-typed literals (ints become Sage Integers, ^ becomes exponentiation), a small version of "deterministic compilation of a human-readable surface". (6) It ships as a library, a local service (Jupyter kernel), and a hosted service (CoCalc), the same three distribution modes the proposed product plans.

## insufficiency

Missing mechanisms, not ambitions: (1) No proof objects — Sage results are computations, not derivations; a backend's answer is trusted as an axiom with no independently checkable certificate, no trace, and no way to distinguish "proved" from "the CAS simplified it". (2) No result taxonomy — failure semantics are untyped Python exceptions or, worse, silently divergent conventions (branch cuts, simplification domains) between Maxima, SymPy and Giac; there is no proved/supported/out_of_regime axis and no disposition axis separating invalid_model from non_convergent from backend death. (3) No provenance receipts per result — reproducibility is "same Sage version ⇒ same answer"; nothing records which backend, which version, what seeds/tolerances produced a given value, and no replay instruction survives outside the monolith. (4) No first-class empirical claims or uncertainty typing — there is a symbolic units module but units, reference frames and model assumptions are not compile-time obligations; nothing is fail-closed. (5) The surface is imperative Python, not declarative statements: knowledge is program state, not a queryable, closed-world KB; there is no vocabulary declaration and no fail-closed name resolution (Python NameError is the closest thing). (6) Coercion is heuristic and open-ended ("not all parent pairs yield a common structure"; parents "not always as tight as possible" per the reference docs), so semantic unification is best-effort, not verified. (7) GPL licensing and the C/Cython in-process entanglement make it unusable as a linkable component of a permissively-licensed product.

## narrowing_history

It narrowed, with an unusually well-documented paper trail. Original 2005 mission: replace ALL of Magma, Maple, Mathematica and Matlab. By August 2014 Stein himself wrote on sage-devel that, measured against that mission statement, Sage had "overall failed" (community members disputed the framing but conceded scientific-computing weakness). The narrowing had three fronts: (a) the Matlab/scientific-computing ambition was ceded to the NumPy/SciPy/Jupyter stack — Sage now bundles them rather than competing; Sage's de facto niche is pure mathematics (number theory, algebra, combinatorics), where reviewers in the 2014 thread said it genuinely competes with Magma; (b) the Sage Notebook (once a flagship) was abandoned for Jupyter, and the hosted ambition was spun out commercially as SageMathCloud→CoCalc after Stein's repeated NSF grant failures (his 2015 blog and sage-devel posts document "no NSF funding for Sage" and development "on a shoestring"); (c) Sage-the-distribution is now being deliberately dismantled: issue #39192 (Dec 2024, still open) proposes futures ranging from "minimal spkg set" to "eliminate custom scripts entirely", and the Meson/pyproject.toml migration plus modularized PyPI distributions (sagemath-standard etc.) replace the vendored monolith with reliance on system/conda packages. Cause of narrowing, per the sources: maintenance cost of the integration surface outran volunteer capacity, and funding never matched the mission's scope.

## license

Sage library: GPL-2.0-or-later ("GNU General Public License GPLv2+" per the repository README, with included packages "under compatible open source licenses" detailed in COPYING.txt; sagemath.org says simply "licensed under the GPL"). The shipped artifact is an aggregate of ~90+ components under assorted GPL-compatible licenses — several key backends (e.g. Maxima, GAP, Singular) are themselves GPL, which is partly why the whole is copyleft. Distribution model: build-from-source "Sage-the-distribution", prebuilt binaries, conda-forge, modularized pip distributions (sagemath-standard and split packages), Docker images, and hosted via the commercial CoCalc. Consequence for the proposed MIT/Apache-2.0 product: SageMath code cannot be linked in; only its architecture and its arm's-length backends with permissive licenses can be adopted.

## adoption

Genuinely large and alive, but volunteer-run. Observable evidence from this task: current stable 10.9 with 10.10 betas landing through August 2026 (10.10.beta9 dated Aug 23 on the releases page; note the fetch summarizer printed 2024 for these while search results date 10.9 to May 2026 and gitlab tags show 10.8 betas in late 2025 — the cadence of roughly one stable + ~10 betas per cycle is consistent either way); 141,350 commits; 5,000+ open issues and ~632 open PRs; packaged in Debian (after a years-long removal for unmaintainability) and major distros; PyPI presence via modularized distributions; used pervasively in university teaching and pure-math research (the 2014 sage-devel thread and FAQ document classroom and research adoption). Funding reality: historically NSF grants (lapsed by 2015 per Stein's own account) and the EU Horizon 2020 OpenDreamKit project (€7.6M, 2015–2019, ~11 FTE across the ecosystem — SageMath, GAP, PARI, Singular, Jupyter); today GitHub Sponsors/donations with no dedicated paid core team; the one commercial entity (SageMath Inc./CoCalc) monetizes hosting, not engine development.

## lessons

Copy: (1) the coercion/parent discipline — a single semantic spine into which every backend's objects must be canonically mapped, with an explicit distinction between automatic canonical coercion and explicit lossy conversion; the proposed product's typed IR should adopt that canonical-morphism rule and make pushout failure a typed compile error rather than a heuristic miss. (2) Doctests-as-conformance-corpus at scale: hundreds of thousands of executable examples are what let Sage survive backend upgrades at all; a solver orchestrator needs the per-solver-version equivalent as a gate. (3) Pinning exact backend versions for reproducibility — but record them per-result (receipts), not per-distribution. Avoid: (1) becoming a distribution — vendoring and patching ~400+ packages with thousands of lines of install scripts consumed the project's volunteer capacity and is now being reversed after ~20 years; keep solvers out-of-process, discovered not built. (2) Text/subprocess (pexpect-style) interfaces without typed contracts — they are where Sage's semantic mismatches (branch cuts, simplification conventions, equality models) leak through. (3) Copyleft, if a permissive linkable library is the goal — Sage's GPL aggregate forecloses embedding. (4) Defining the mission as "replace X, Y, Z" — Sage's founder judged it a failure against that framing even while the project thrived in its real niche; scope the mission to the verifiable-orchestration mechanism, not to incumbent displacement.

## cluster_answer

The integration model's real cost was that Sage became an operating-system-scale distribution as a side effect of being a math library. Concretely: the wrapper/unification layer grew to 700,000+ lines of Python/Cython whose largest single subsystem (coercion) exists solely to reconcile the backends' incompatible type/equality models via canonical morphisms and heuristic pushouts; the distribution layer accumulated ~442 SPKGs, patches carried against ~63 of them, and ~4,300 lines of spkg-install scripts (metrics cited in the Sage packaging pages/issue discussion) that "require constant updates as dependencies evolve" with "no automated mechanism", making dependency installation "a common source of build errors requiring developer time" (issue #39192, Dec 2024); multi-hour source builds; Debian removed Sage entirely for years because it was "one of the most complex packages in Debian" and unmaintainable, and downstream packagers routinely "start by neutering the package system"; no native Windows support for nearly two decades (only now opened by the Meson migration). Version pinning was all-or-nothing: the monolith pinned everything, so reproducibility was bought at the price of permanent staleness pressure and a CI burden the project explicitly names as a maintenance cost. Semantic mismatches were never eliminated, only wrapped: Maxima/SymPy/Giac disagree on simplification domains and branch cuts, and the coercion docs concede the model is heuristic and incomplete. Prediction for a solver-orchestrator: interface maintenance scales as (number of backends) × (their release cadence), and the adapters — not the core engine — become the dominant, permanent engineering surface; you must choose per-backend between pinning (reproducible, stale, build-heavy) and tracking (fresh, breaking), and the only sustainable posture is the one Sage is converging to after 20 years: never vendor backend builds, talk to solvers out-of-process through typed, versioned, conformance-tested contracts, and treat a backend whose semantics can't be captured in the contract as unsupported (fail closed) rather than wrapped. The proposed product's per-result receipts and typed dispositions are precisely the mechanisms Sage lacked to make pinning per-obligation instead of per-universe. Funding/maintenance reality: active weekly-beta development entirely on volunteer labor plus donations; past support was episodic grants (NSF until ~2014, OpenDreamKit €7.6M 2015–2019); no funded core team exists in 2026 — a solo-developer product should assume zero external maintenance of any adapter it ships.

## confidence

## largest_unverified_assumption

That Sage has no mechanism anywhere in its ~90-package surface for per-result provenance, proof certificates, or typed failure dispositions — asserted here from the reference documentation's silence and the architecture's design (results as trusted in-process computations), not from an explicit specification statement; a niche subsystem (e.g. certified numerics via interval arithmetic backends) could partially contradict it without changing the overall conclusion.
