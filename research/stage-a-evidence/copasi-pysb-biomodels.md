## system

COPASI (biochemical network simulator), PySB (Python rule-based model-as-program framework), and the BioModels repository with its manual curation pipeline (plus Tellurium/Antimony as the human-readable-surface precedent)

## overlap

Each subject implements one mechanism the proposed product contains. (1) Antimony (bundled in Tellurium) is the closest precedent for the surface language: a human-readable typed text notation (`S1 -> S2; k1*S1` plus initial conditions) that compiles deterministically to SBML, which functions as the ecosystem's standard IR; phraSED-ML does the same for simulation experiments (SED-ML). (2) PySB is the "models are programs" precedent: models are Python code built from rule-based macros, compiled through BioNetGen into reaction networks/ODEs, version-controllable and unit-testable — the product's declarative-statements-compiled-to-solver-obligations idea, realized as an embedded DSL rather than a standalone compiler. (3) COPASI is the typed external solver the orchestrator would delegate to: ODE, SDE, and Gillespie stochastic simulation with discrete events, steady-state analysis, and parameter estimation via multiple population-based optimizers (Differential Evolution, SRES, Genetic Algorithm, Particle Swarm, Scatter Search), with SBML import/export. (4) The BioModels/MIRIAM/BioSimulators stack is the vocabulary supply chain plus reproducibility-receipt system: curators annotate every model element with cross-references to controlled vocabularies (GO, ChEBI, UniProt, NCBI Taxonomy) under the MIRIAM guidelines; SED-ML files are machine-readable replay instructions; COMBINE archives bundle model+experiment; and the 2025 verification effort ran 1,055 curated ODE models through five independent engines (COPASI, Tellurium, VCell, PySCeS, Amici) wrapped in Docker containers with pinned identities, comparing HDF5 numerical outputs — solver identity, version pinning, and cross-engine numerical comparison, which is most of the product's "reproducibility receipt" concept implemented at community scale for one domain.

## insufficiency

Missing mechanisms, not ambitions: (a) No logical inference anywhere in the stack — SBML/COPASI/PySB produce trajectories and fitted parameters, never entailments; there are no proof objects, no verdict axis (a model neither "proves" nor "refutes" a claim; it plots a curve a human compares to a figure). (b) No typed uncertainty: parameters are point estimates; measurement/numerical/parameter/structural uncertainty are not distinguished as first-class typed claims (the SBML Distributions package exists but is marginal — inference, not verified here). (c) Provenance is advisory metadata, not hash-bound obligation: the 2025 verification paper found curated SED-ML files pointing at nonexistent "model.xml" files, SBML parameters set to Infinity/NaN, and stale annotation URLs — precisely because references were names, not content hashes, and nothing failed closed at ingest. (d) Vocabulary grounding (MIRIAM annotation) happens after modeling, by human curators, not at compile time; undeclared vocabulary is not a compile error in any of these tools. (e) No typed result taxonomy: a failed run is a tool-specific error string; nothing distinguishes non_convergent from out_of_regime from invalid_model. (f) Domain scope is biochemistry only — no mathematics/physics generality, no reference frames, no symbolic math beyond what SymPy provides inside PySB. (g) PySB inherits Python-environment nondeterminism and requires Perl+BioNetGen; deterministic compilation is not a contract it states or tests.

## narrowing_history

COPASI and PySB did not narrow: COPASI has been a biochemical-network simulator continuously (4.46 released 2026-01-23, still adding parameter-estimation features); PySB's scope is stable since its 2013 Molecular Systems Biology paper. BioModels is the instructive case, and its history is a widening that failed to carry assurance, forcing a de facto narrowing of the trusted core: the Path2Models project auto-generated >140,000 models from KEGG/BioCarta/MetaCyc/SABIO-RK pathway maps (2013), so the repository split into a small curated branch and a huge machine-generated branch — but the curated branch grew only from ~800 models (2020, of ~2,000 literature submissions) to 1,055 verified ODE models (2025), because the load-bearing curation step — numerically reproducing the published figures — "cannot be easily automated" (2025 PLOS Comp Biol paper's own words). The trusted product of twenty years of institutional curation is ~1,000 models; volume was achievable only by dropping verification. Separately, the SED-ML/BioSimulators/COMBINE layer is itself a retrofit: a decade of community effort to add replay instructions and pinned solver identities that the original model-file-only pipeline lacked, culminating in the 2025 repair campaign that had to fix the curated corpus's own receipts.

## lessons

Copy: (1) the Antimony pattern — readable surface compiling to a standard, tool-independent IR is what let five independent engines verify the same corpus; the product's IR should be as solver-neutral as SBML. (2) The BioSimulators verification mechanism — containerized solvers with pinned identities and cross-engine numerical comparison of HDF5 outputs is a working implementation of "reproducibility receipts" and the strongest known defense against single-solver bugs; orchestrate COPASI/Tellurium/Amici rather than re-implementing ODE/SSA/parameter-estimation engines. (3) SED-ML's separation of model from experiment (what to simulate, with what tolerances) as a distinct typed artifact. (4) PySB's models-as-code discipline: models under version control with tests. Avoid: the ecosystem's original sin — advisory, name-based, after-the-fact provenance. 49% of published models were irreproducible from their papers (Tiwari 2021, 455 models), and even the manually curated corpus accumulated dangling file references, NaN parameters, and stale annotation URLs that a 2025 repair campaign had to fix — because nothing was hash-bound and nothing failed closed at ingest. The product's fail-closed compilation and hash-bound receipts are exactly the mechanisms whose absence created twenty years of curation debt. Second mistake: letting auto-generated volume (140k Path2Models entries) sit beside verified content without a hard typed distinction — the product's verdict/disposition axes must apply to the corpus itself.

## cluster_answer

BioModels' numbers: ~2,000 literature-derived models of which ~800 were manually curated in 2020, growing to 1,055 curated ODE models by the 2025 verification study, versus >140,000 uncurated auto-generated Path2Models entries — i.e., two decades of institutional curation at EMBL-EBI produced a trusted core of only ~1,000 models. Curator headcount is not published as a discrete figure (the 15-years paper lists ~25 authors across the team; evidence-incomplete). Curation checks three things: (1) re-encoding into SBML if submitted otherwise, (2) cross-verification of the model structure against the paper, (3) numerical reproduction of the publication's simulation figures, plus MIRIAM-compliant annotation of every element to controlled vocabularies (GO, ChEBI, UniProt, NCBI Taxonomy). The governance lesson: the expensive step is reproducing published results — it "cannot be easily automated," it is the throughput bottleneck (51% of 455 published models reproduced without curator intervention per Tiwari 2021), and curated content decays anyway (the 2025 study found broken model references, Infinity/NaN parameters, and stale annotation URLs inside the curated corpus, and only 88% verified end-to-end after repair). A vocabulary/model supply chain therefore needs either permanent funded human curation or machine-checkable, hash-bound, fail-closed receipts at ingest — BioModels had to retrofit the latter (SED-ML + BioSimulators) after the fact. What COPASI/PySB deliver that the product should not re-implement: COPASI gives production-grade ODE/SDE/Gillespie simulation with discrete events, steady-state analysis, and a battery of parameter-estimation optimizers behind SBML; PySB gives rule-based model construction as testable, version-controlled code compiling through BioNetGen; Tellurium/libroadrunner gives a second fast independent engine, enabling cross-engine verification. What they lack, which is the product's actual territory: logical inference with proof objects and a verdict taxonomy; typed uncertainty (measurement vs numerical vs parameter vs structural); fail-closed unit/vocabulary/assumption checking at compile time; hash-bound provenance; and a typed disposition taxonomy for failures.
