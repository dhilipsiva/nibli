## subject

Mathematics substrate requirements for the four-wide IR (Stage B, prompt section 4): exact/validated number representations, symbolic and structural mathematics, FOL-vs-HOL boundary, six integration strategies, Nibli fragment characterization, Rust crate reality check

## findings

**Number tower.** Integers/rationals must be arbitrary-precision ground kinds (ℤ, ℚ) with exact equality; every mature ecosystem (GMP, FLINT, num, dashu, malachite) provides this and there is no design controversy. ALGEBRAIC NUMBERS have three candidate representations. (1) Minimal polynomial + isolating interval: FLINT's `qqbar_t` stores the unique reduced minimal polynomial in ℤ[x] plus a complex isolating ball whose precision is maintained automatically "to ensure that all operations are exact"; equality is decidable (`qqbar_equal`), as are rationality, realness, root-of-unity tests (FLINT 3.7.0-dev docs, accessed 2026-08-26). (2) Symbolic radical towers: non-canonical (many spellings of one number), cannot represent general roots of degree ≥ 5 polynomials (Abel–Ruffini), and equality requires normalization that can blow up — fine as *surface syntax*, wrong as canonical IR. (3) Ball-approximation-with-certified-polynomial is what (1) already is operationally; a bare ball without the polynomial loses exactness and decidable equality. FLINT's Calcium (`ca`) module goes further — exact real/complex numbers as elements of formal extension fields with equality decidable over algebraics and *partially* decidable (may return Unknown) over transcendentals — and is alive but explicitly "experimental... interfaces subject to change" inside FLINT (Arb, Antic, Calcium were merged into FLINT 3.0 in 2023; the standalone repos are archived; current FLINT release 3.6.0, 2026-06-29, LGPL-3.0+). Recommendation: qqbar model canonical; Calcium's Unknown-returning equality is precedent for the product's `unknown` verdict on transcendental identity.

**Arbitrary-precision floating point** is a distinct kind from ℚ (rounding is semantic, not noise). MPFR (4.2.2, LGPL-3.0+) is the normative semantics: precision is an attribute of *each variable* ("can be set exactly to any valid value for each variable"), rounding mode is a *per-operation parameter* (RNDN/RNDD/RNDU/RNDZ/RNDA plus faithful), and the contract is correct rounding: "compute the requested operation exactly... and round the result to the precision of the destination variable, with the given rounding mode" (MPFR manual, accessed 2026-08-26). The IR type should be `Float{prec: u32, rnd: RoundingMode}` — both in the type, never global mutable state (MPFR itself has a global default precision; do not replicate that). This makes every FP result reproducible bit-for-bit and every claimed digit a typed claim.

**Modular arithmetic and finite fields** are needed even with no crypto workflow, because they are the *certificate machinery* for exact linear algebra — the product's stoichiometric core. Concrete schemes: Freivalds' O(n²) randomized verification of matrix products; Dumas–Kaltofen "essentially optimal interactive certificates in linear algebra" (ISSAC 2014, arXiv:1401.4567) for determinant, rank, minimal/characteristic polynomial and positive semidefiniteness, verifiable in (n² log‖A‖)^{1+o(1)} Monte-Carlo bit complexity; Dumas–Kaltofen–Thomé–Villard linear-time interactive certificates for sparse determinant/minpoly (arXiv:1507.01083); computation-side, CRT reconstruction and Dixon p-adic lifting solve rational systems fraction-free with cheap residue re-checks. Caveat for the taxonomy: these checks are *probabilistic* — whether a Monte-Carlo-verified result may carry verdict `proved` or must land in `supported` with an error-probability annotation is an owner decision (see open questions).

**Intervals/validated numerics.** IEEE 1788-2015 was administratively moved to **Inactive-Reserved status on 2026-03-26** (10-year no-revision rule; IEEE SA status page, accessed 2026-08-26); its subset IEEE 1788.1-2017 (binary64 endpoints + decoration system for exception-free computation) is the practically implemented profile. Adoptable? Yes — as *frozen semantics to transcribe*, which actually suits transcription-only governance: the text will not move underneath. The Rust crate inari (2.0.0, MIT, last release 2024-08) "conforms to IEEE Std 1788.1-2017" and implements a subset of 1788-2015. Arb-style mid-rad balls (arf midpoint at working precision + low-precision mag radius, guarantee: output ball contains the exact result for any points of the input balls) are the right representation for *arbitrary-precision* enclosures and special functions; inf-sup/1788 intervals are the right one for double-precision propagation and for the decoration system, whose `trv/def/dac/com` decorations map naturally onto the product's typed partiality and out_of_regime. Ship both as distinct enclosure kinds; both are proved-with-certificate carriers (the enclosure is the certificate, re-checkable by replay). **Complex numbers**: pairs over each real kind (ℚ(i), qqbar is already complex, acb balls, complex 1788 rectangles) — no separate design problem beyond branch-cut discipline in the operator library, which must be declared per-operator (principal branch, domain-of-definition), not documented prose.

**Symbolic layer.** Expressions/functions/binders: a small typed term calculus — application, *binding as first-class syntax* (OpenMath's object model: applications/bindings/attributions over symbols referencing content dictionaries — the pattern to transcribe; the CD corpus itself is dormant since 2022 and semantics-free per Stage A, so transcribe the mechanism, not the vocabulary). Sums/products/limits/derivatives/integrals are binder-applications with typed partiality: `∂/∂t`, `∫_a^b`, `lim` are operators whose *unevaluated form is a first-class value* (Gate 1 explicitly permits this) and whose evaluation is a separate judgment carrying a regime (differentiability class, integrability) — never silently evaluated. Sets/sequences: finite sets/maps as data; comprehensions only over decidable predicates and bounded domains (fail-closed otherwise). Equations/inequalities: first-class relational objects (not booleans) so they can be manipulated, solved, and cited. Matrices/tensors/linear operators: shape-indexed types `Matrix[m,n,K]`/`Tensor[shape,K]` with index arithmetic checked at compile time — encoding them as generic predicates is a named fidelity failure. Algebraic structures: a typeclass-like hierarchy (mathlib precedent: fail-closed instance resolution at 135k+ definitions proves viability) but shallow — only the structures operators need (group/ring/field/module/order). Rewrite systems: equality saturation with e-graphs (egg 0.11.0, MIT, 3.0M downloads) with the rewrite trace recorded as an independently checkable certificate. Constraint solving: SMT-LIB2 as the wire format; QF_LRA/QF_LIA cover stoichiometric feasibility and regime guards. Theorem statements and proof terms: statements are IR objects; proofs are *content-addressed references to external artifacts* plus checker identity/version (LRAT for SAT, Alethe checked by Carcara — Rust, Apache-2.0 — for SMT; lean4export/leanchecker for assistant-grade audits), consistent with Stage A's finding that assistant-internal proof terms are not consumable.

**Where FOL suffices.** All five primary workflows are FOL-or-below: stoichiometric linear algebra is computation over ℚ plus certificates (no quantifier reasoning); unit algebra is a decidable equational theory (free abelian group on base dimensions with integer exponents — normalization, not proof search); correlated uncertainty propagation is matrix computation bound to GUM; finite logical inference is Datalog/FOL; regime checking is FOL guards over linear/nonlinear arithmetic (SMT-decidable fragments). Shape- and unit-indexed types need only *value-indexed* types with decidable index equality (linear integer arithmetic on exponents/dimensions) — lightweight, not full dependent types. The first competency question that genuinely requires higher-order reasoning: **"for a reaction network with stoichiometric matrix N, show that every left null vector w (wᵀN = 0) yields a conserved quantity — wᵀx(t) constant for all solutions x(t) of the induced ODE"** — this quantifies over the function space of solutions. Checking wᵀN = 0 for a *given* w is FOL/computation; the "for all solutions" half is a theorem about ODE semantics. Likewise "the first-order GUM propagation formula is correct for every differentiable f" quantifies over functions, and proving a summation identity *for all n* needs induction (a second-order schema; fixed instances stay FO). Ruling: the kernel states such claims as typed theorem-statement objects and discharges them by reference to external proof artifacts; it never implements HO proof search.

**Six integration strategies.** (1) *Native exact arithmetic*: buys determinism, fail-closed totality control, zero TCB expansion, interactive latency; costs implementation effort and a permissive-license constraint on backends; verdict — the substrate for ℤ/ℚ/ℤp/intervals/qqbar-checking, non-negotiable. (2) *SMT integration*: buys decision procedures for regime guards, feasibility, finite verification; costs solver-in-TCB unless proof-producing — adopt only with certificates (cvc5→Alethe→Carcara; Z3 unsat cores insufficient alone); uncertified answers land as `supported`. (3) *Proof-assistant integration*: buys the strongest audit tier and an export target (Lean statements elaborate in seconds; Apache-2.0 throughout; leanchecker/lean4lean give independent re-checking) but mathlib's ℝ is noncomputable, latency is human-scale, and kernels are not Rust-linkable — authoring/audit-side only, never the runtime, per Stage A. (4) *Computer-algebra systems*: buy symbolic breadth (integration, closed forms); cost unsound-by-default simplification (branch cuts, 0^0), no certificates, licensing (Mathematica proprietary; Symbolica 2.2.0 is "non-standard" license per crates.io); use answer-then-verify — CAS proposes, product re-checks (differentiate the antiderivative; polyrith's re-certification is the precedent). (5) *Independently checkable certificates*: the spine consistent with Stage A ("enclosures are proved-with-certificate") — per-format small Rust checkers (LRAT, Alethe, Freivalds/Dumas–Kaltofen replay, isolating-interval sign checks, SOS identities, subdivision traces); cost is per-format checker maintenance and the randomized-verification taxonomy question. (6) *Opaque symbolic backends*: buy capability breadth immediately; cost verdict laundering — quarantine to `supported` with backend identity recorded in the evidence graph, never `proved`; the Nibli compute-backend disclosure ("part of the TCB for that proof step") is the honest precedent and the ceiling.

**Nibli's fragment (prose only).** From LOGIC_IR.md: a 13-node FOL buffer (Predicate/And/Or/Not/Exists/ForAll, tense/deontic wrappers, query-only CountNode) whose term language (5 variants) has *no function application* — surface-level function-free FOL; GUARANTEES §Negation Policy notes the compiled program is "not function-free" internally only because rule heads carry dependent Skolems. Semantics: stratified negation-as-failure under closed-world + closed-domain (§Negation Policy: rejection of negative cycles at registration, Lean-proved criterion; §Query Result Contract: FALSE = "not derivable"); depth-bounded backward chaining with stratum-ordered materialisation. Arithmetic status: numbers are `f64` literals with *bitwise* store equality (−0.0 ≠ 0.0, §Disclosed Sharp Edges); product/sum/quotient are query-only proof-local compute with *tolerant* equality (isclose 1e-9); comparisons (`greater`/`less`/`num_equal`) are query-only and **refused in every rule position** ("Numeric threshold rules... is a compile error"); §What the Engine Cannot Do: "First-order logic only", no uncertainty propagation, no arithmetic on time. So Nibli covers exactly the *finite logical inference* lane — function-free stratified-NAF FOL with certificates (proof traces, Lean-proved core) — and nothing of the numeric substrate; its `Number(f64)` term and tolerant equality are disqualifying for exact mathematics and must not be inherited.

**Rust reality check** (all crates.io/GitHub, accessed 2026-08-26): rug 1.30.0 — LGPL-3.0+, wraps GMP 6.3.0 (dual LGPLv3/GPLv2) + MPFR 4.2.2 (LGPL-3.0+), active (2026-04). malachite 0.10.0 — **LGPL-3.0-only**, pure Rust, very active (2026-07), 8.8M downloads. dashu 0.6.0 — MIT OR Apache-2.0, pure Rust GMP+MPFR+MPC alternative, updated 2026-08-09. num-bigint 0.4.8 — MIT OR Apache-2.0, 548M downloads, 2026-07. inari 2.0.0 — MIT, IEEE 1788.1-2017-conformant, needs directed rounding (x86-64 Haswell+/AArch64). astro-float 0.9.6 — MIT, pure-Rust MPFR-style, 2026-08. flint-sys 0.9.0 — MIT OR Apache-2.0 bindings (to LGPL FLINT). ark-ff 0.6.0 — MIT OR Apache-2.0 finite fields (crypto-tuned, 88M downloads). egg 0.11.0 — MIT, e-graphs. symbolica 2.2.0 — non-standard license, avoid linking. LGPL crates statically linked into a Rust MIT/Apache product create a real compliance burden (LGPL §4 relink obligation): keep GMP/MPFR/FLINT-backed paths behind an off-by-default cargo feature; permissive pure-Rust as the default build.

## design_recommendations

- **decision**: Ground exact-number kinds | **recommendation**: Make ℤ and ℚ arbitrary-precision ground kinds with exact structural equality; default backend permissive pure-Rust (num-bigint 0.4.8/num-rational, or dashu 0.6.0), with an off-by-default cargo feature swapping in GMP-backed rug/FLINT for performance. | **rationale**: MIT OR Apache-2.0 distribution as a linkable library makes statically-linked LGPL (rug 1.30.0 LGPL-3.0+, malachite 0.10.0 LGPL-3.0-only, FLINT 3.6.0 LGPL-3.0+) a downstream compliance burden; num/dashu are permissive and active (fetched 2026-08-26). Performance parity is a feature flag, not a license default. | **alternatives_rejected**: malachite as default (fastest pure-Rust but LGPL-3.0-only); rug as default (LGPL + C toolchain requirement); f64 anywhere in the exact tower (Nibli's disclosed −0.0/0.0 and isclose lessons).
- **decision**: Canonical algebraic-number representation | **recommendation**: Adopt the qqbar model: unique reduced minimal polynomial over ℤ plus certified isolating interval/ball, with decidable equality; the (minpoly, isolating box) pair doubles as an independently checkable certificate (checker replays sign-change/interval-Newton containment). | **rationale**: FLINT qqbar proves the model at production quality (exactness maintained automatically, equality decidable — FLINT docs 2026-08-26); it is canonical (one representation per number) and total over all algebraics. | **alternatives_rejected**: Symbolic radical towers as canonical form (non-canonical, cannot express general degree-≥5 roots, normalization blowup — keep only as surface syntax that compiles to qqbar); bare ball approximation (loses exactness/decidable equality); Calcium ca fields as v1 canonical form (explicitly experimental in FLINT 3.x, though its Unknown-returning transcendental equality is the precedent for verdict unknown on transcendental identity).
- **decision**: Arbitrary-precision float kind with rounding in the type | **recommendation**: Define Float{prec, rounding_mode} as a kind distinct from ℚ, with MPFR semantics normative: per-variable precision, per-operation rounding mode (RNDN/RNDD/RNDU/RNDZ/RNDA), correct rounding ('compute exactly, then round to destination precision') as the operation contract; no global precision/rounding state anywhere in the IR or runtime. | **rationale**: MPFR 4.2.2 manual (2026-08-26) defines exactly this and is the de-facto oracle; putting prec+rnd in the type makes every emitted digit a typed, reproducible claim and satisfies the fail-closed rule (a float without declared precision/rounding is a compile error). | **alternatives_rejected**: Global rounding/precision settings (MPFR's own global default is the anti-pattern; irreproducible); rationals-as-floats (conflates exact and rounded claims); IEEE 754 fixed formats only (insufficient for validated tightening loops).
- **decision**: Two enclosure kinds: 1788.1 intervals and mid-rad balls | **recommendation**: Ship BOTH: (a) an inf-sup interval kind transcribing IEEE 1788.1-2017 semantics including decorations, implemented over inari 2.0.0 (MIT); (b) an Arb-style mid-rad ball kind (arbitrary-precision midpoint, low-precision radius, containment guarantee) behind the FLINT feature flag for arbitrary-precision and special functions. Map 1788 decorations onto typed partiality/out_of_regime. | **rationale**: IEEE 1788-2015 went Inactive-Reserved 2026-03-26 — frozen text, which suits transcription-only governance (a mechanizable, non-moving oracle); 1788.1 is the implemented profile (inari conforms). Balls are strictly better for high-precision enclosures (Arb containment guarantee, FLINT docs); intervals are better for binary64 propagation and carry the decoration system 1788 defines and balls lack. | **alternatives_rejected**: 1788-2015 full standard as target (no complete Rust implementation; inactive status means no upstream maintenance of the full text); balls only (loses decorations and binary64 interop); intervals only (poor at high precision; no special-function library with certified bounds comparable to Arb).
- **decision**: Finite fields and modular arithmetic as first-class IR kinds | **recommendation**: Include ℤ/nℤ, ℤ/pℤ and F_q in the IR, motivated as certificate machinery: Freivalds verification for matrix identities, Dumas–Kaltofen interactive certificates (determinant/rank/minpoly/charpoly/PSD, ISSAC 2014) and the sparse variants (Dumas–Kaltofen–Thomé–Villard), CRT/Dixon-lifting exact rational solving with residue re-checks. Implement natively (Montgomery arithmetic is small); ark-ff 0.6.0 (MIT OR Apache-2.0) available if a general F_q library is wanted. | **rationale**: The primary workflow (stoichiometric exact linear algebra) needs cheap verification of expensive exact computations; modular images are how every named scheme achieves it, and the schemes are published with verified complexity bounds (arXiv:1401.4567, 1507.01083, fetched via search 2026-08-26). | **alternatives_rejected**: Recompute-to-verify (defeats the certificate architecture, O(n^3)); omitting finite fields from the IR and hiding them in the checker (violates four-domain-wide fidelity — F_q is legitimate mathematics a user may state).
- **decision**: Symbolic expressions with first-class binders, transcribed from the OpenMath object model | **recommendation**: A small typed term calculus — application, binding (binder + bound variables + body), attribution — where derivative/integral/sum/product/limit are binder-operators whose unevaluated form is a first-class value and whose evaluation is a separate typed judgment with declared domain-of-definition and branch choice. Transcribe the OpenMath mechanism (symbol-grounded vocabulary, structural fail-closed resolution), not its content dictionaries. | **rationale**: Gate 1 permits first-class unevaluated forms and typed partiality; OpenMath proves the object model but its CD corpus is dormant (last commits 2022) and compliance is encoding-level, never semantic (Stage A openmath-mathml.md); binder-as-syntax keeps the term language first-order-representable. | **alternatives_rejected**: Encoding binders via combinators/strings (fidelity failure); adopting OpenMath CDs as vocabulary (dormant, semantics-free, fails the mechanizable-oracle governance test); full lambda calculus with HO unification in the kernel (imports undecidability the workflows never need).
- **decision**: Shape-indexed matrices/tensors via value-indexed types, not dependent types | **recommendation**: Matrix[m,n,K] / Tensor[shape,K] with compile-time index arithmetic in decidable linear integer arithmetic — the same machinery as unit-exponent vectors (free abelian group on base dimensions). Never generic predicates for tensors, equations, functions, or molecular graphs (Gate 1). | **rationale**: Shape errors and unit errors are the same class of bug and the same decidable theory; mathlib demonstrates fail-closed instance/index resolution at scale, and the AFP Physical_Quantities entry demonstrates dimensions-as-type-level-exponent-vectors (Stage A). Full dependent types are not required by any primary workflow. | **alternatives_rejected**: Full dependent type theory in the kernel (proof-obligation latency, Lean's authoring-cost wall — Stage A lean-mathlib.md); untyped dynamic shape checks (fail-open, violates fidelity test); predicate encoding of matrices (named Gate 1 failure).
- **decision**: FOL kernel + external-artifact discharge for higher-order claims | **recommendation**: Kernel reasoning stays FOL + decidable theories (the five primary workflows need nothing more). Higher-order statements — first triggered by the conservation-law competency question 'every left null vector w of N gives wᵀx(t) constant for all ODE solutions x' — are representable as typed theorem-statement objects and dischargeable only by reference to an external proof artifact (Lean statement export; leanchecker/lean4lean re-checking), recorded in the evidence graph with checker identity. | **rationale**: Quantification over function spaces and induction schemas are where FOL ends; Stage A established assistants are audit-tier, not runtime, and Lean statements elaborate in seconds while proofs cost person-months — so statements in, proofs by reference. | **alternatives_rejected**: HO proof search in the kernel (adoption ceiling demonstrated by 40 years of assistants — Stage A); silently downgrading HO claims to FO approximations (fidelity failure); refusing to represent HO statements at all (the IR must state what it cannot prove — that is what verdict/disposition separation is for).
- **decision**: Rewrite/simplification via equality saturation with recorded traces | **recommendation**: Use e-graph equality saturation (egg 0.11.0, MIT, 3.0M downloads) as the simplification engine; every applied rewrite rule is a named, cited axiom of a declared theory, and the extraction trace is a replayable certificate. Guard-conditioned rewrites (x/x→1 requires x≠0) are typed partiality, enforced, not documented. | **rationale**: Equality saturation is non-destructive (keeps all equivalent forms — matches the evidence-graph philosophy), the Rust implementation is the field's reference, and traced rewriting turns simplification from an opaque CAS step into checked inference. | **alternatives_rejected**: CAS-style untraced simplify (branch-cut unsoundness, no certificate); hand-rolled ordered term rewriting (confluence/termination proofs become the product's problem; e-graphs sidestep ordering).
- **decision**: Constraint solving via SMT-LIB2 with certificates-required tiering | **recommendation**: SMT-LIB2 as the solver wire format (QF_LRA/QF_LIA for stoichiometric feasibility and regime guards; QF_NRA where needed); a solver answer upgrades to proved only when it ships a checkable certificate (Alethe checked by Carcara in-process, Apache-2.0; LRAT for propositional cores); otherwise it lands as supported with solver identity/version/seed in the evidence graph. | **rationale**: Consistent with Stage A's Sledgehammer lesson (untrusted oracles, checked reconstruction) and the two-axis taxonomy; keeps the TCB at the small Rust checkers rather than at each solver. | **alternatives_rejected**: Trusting solver verdicts on identity (Nibli's own compute-backend disclosure shows where that ends: backend joins the TCB); building native decision procedures for all theories (years of work replicating cvc5/Z3).
- **decision**: Randomized-certificate verdict policy (owner ruling needed) | **recommendation**: Classify Monte-Carlo-verified results (Freivalds, interactive linear-algebra certificates) as a distinct certificate class carrying an explicit error-probability bound; recommend they map to proved only when the protocol is derandomized or the failure probability is bounded below a declared threshold recorded in the certificate — otherwise supported{margin}. | **rationale**: The named verification schemes are probabilistically sound (Monte-Carlo bit complexity, arXiv:1401.4567); the two-axis taxonomy has no slot for 'proved except with probability 2^-128' today, and silently rounding that to proved would be the first crack in the zero-defaulting stance. | **alternatives_rejected**: Treating randomized checks as deterministic proofs (unsound as stated); banning them (forfeits the only sub-cubic verification known for exact linear algebra).
- **decision**: Nibli reuse boundary | **recommendation**: Reuse Nibli only for the finite-logical-inference lane (stratified-NAF function-free FOL, closed world, proof traces, Lean-proved core, differential oracles); the mathematics substrate must be a new IR layer — do not extend LogicalTerm::Number(f64) or the query-only compute path into exact mathematics. | **rationale**: From prose only: LOGIC_IR.md terms are function-free with f64 literals and bitwise store equality; GUARANTEES discloses tolerant isclose arithmetic, query-only comparisons refused in rule positions, 'first-order logic only', no uncertainty propagation. Its logic engine is exactly the product's finite-inference component; its number story is exactly what the substrate exists to replace. | **alternatives_rejected**: Growing Nibli's term language into the math substrate (its own GUARANTEES document the f64/tolerant-equality contract as load-bearing and pinned; retrofitting exact kinds would break every disclosed sharp edge); ignoring Nibli entirely (its stratified-NAF + certificate discipline is directly reusable and already Lean-proved).

## adopt

MPFR 4.2.2 correct-rounding semantics (per-variable precision, per-operation rounding mode) as the normative float contract; IEEE 1788.1-2017 interval semantics + decorations via inari 2.0.0 (MIT); FLINT 3.6.0 (2026-06-29) qqbar model — minimal polynomial + certified isolating interval — as the canonical algebraic-number representation (FLINT itself behind an LGPL feature flag); num-bigint 0.4.8 / dashu 0.6.0 (MIT OR Apache-2.0) as default ℤ/ℚ backends; egg 0.11.0 (MIT) equality saturation with traced rewrites; SMT-LIB2 + Alethe/Carcara (Apache-2.0) certified solver integration; Freivalds + Dumas–Kaltofen interactive certificates (ISSAC 2014, arXiv:1401.4567) over ℤ/pℤ for exact linear algebra verification; ark-ff 0.6.0 (MIT OR Apache-2.0) if a general finite-field library is needed; Lean 4 statement-level export (Apache-2.0, lean4export/leanchecker) as the higher-order discharge target.

## avoid

f64 as a ground exact-number type and tolerant (isclose) equality — Nibli's own GUARANTEES document the resulting sharp edges (−0.0/0.0 as two members, bitwise store equality); global precision/rounding state (irreproducible results, MPFR's global default is the anti-pattern); symbolic radical towers as the canonical algebraic representation (non-canonical, incomplete past degree 4, normalization blowup); LGPL crates (rug, malachite, FLINT) in the default build of an MIT OR Apache-2.0 linkable library — statically linked LGPL imposes relink obligations on every downstream; wholesale adoption of OpenMath CDs (dormant since 2022, compliance is encoding-level not semantic); reflection-only numeric proofs à la CoqInterval (no transferable certificate — Stage A); opaque CAS/backend results mapped to proved (verdict laundering; Symbolica 2.2.0 additionally has a non-standard license); encoding tensors, equations, functions, or molecular graphs as generic predicates (named Gate 1 fidelity failure); treating IEEE 1788-2015 full conformance as a target (Inactive-Reserved since 2026-03-26, no complete Rust implementation — target the 1788.1 profile instead).

## open_questions

- Do any permissively-licensed pure-Rust float libraries (astro-float 0.9.6, dashu-float) actually guarantee MPFR-grade correct rounding for transcendental functions, or is the LGPL feature flag (MPFR/rug) unavoidable for certified transcendentals? (unverified in-session)
- Owner ruling: may a Monte-Carlo-verified certificate (Freivalds, Dumas–Kaltofen interactive) carry verdict proved with a recorded error bound, or must it land in supported{margin}? This decides the exact-linear-algebra verification architecture.
- Formal mapping of IEEE 1788.1 decorations (com/dac/def/trv) onto the product's typed partiality and out_of_regime — is a decoration-to-disposition lift lossless, and who owns it?
- Is Calcium (FLINT ca, explicitly experimental) stable enough by implementation time to back partially-decidable transcendental equality, or should transcendental identity always return unknown in v1?
- LGPL compliance posture for the linkable-library distribution: is dynamic linking of GMP/MPFR/FLINT acceptable to downstream users, or must the default build be 100% permissive pure-Rust (accepting a performance gap that has not been measured for the stoichiometric workloads)?
- Whether IEEE 1788.1-2017 will itself go Inactive-Reserved (~2027 under the same 10-year rule) and whether that matters for transcription-only governance beyond citation hygiene.
- Sparse exact rational linear algebra in Rust: no maintained equivalent of LinBox was identified — build vs bind decision deferred to implementation-sequencing (chemistry/biology vertical needs sparse stoichiometric matrices).
