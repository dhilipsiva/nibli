## subject

Chemistry domain IR requirements (Stage B, prompt §6): chemical identity, type distinctions, reactions/atom mapping, registries, and balance checking — design inputs for the chemistry half of the first implemented vertical

## findings

IDENTITY LANDSCAPE. InChI is the only chemical identifier with a single normative implementation: the IUPAC/InChI Trust software, currently the 1.07.x line (1.07 approved July 2024, MIT-licensed since 1.07.1; latest release v1.07.5 — GitHub lists it as current; the release-year rendering conflicted across sources, see key_facts). An InChI is layered: main (formula /c connectivity /h hydrogens), charge (/q, /p), stereo (/t tetrahedral+allenes, /b double bond+cumulenes, /m /s), isotopic (/i), and the non-standard fixed-H (/f) and reconnected (/r) layers. Standard InChI (prefix "InChI=1S/") forbids /f and /r and fixes perception options, which is exactly what makes it a database-interchange key — and exactly what makes it lossy: mobile-H normalization collapses tautomers sharing a heteroatom-bounded mobile hydrogen into one identifier (acetamide and acetimidic acid are one Standard InChI), while keto-enol and long-range tautomerism are NOT covered by default (experimental KET/15T options exist and produce non-standard InChIs). Stereo gaps are real: aliphatic amine nitrogen is not treated as stereogenic (aziridines and quaternary N excepted), spin states/radical types are indistinguishable, and unknown stereo is conflated with undefined in standard mode; coverage of atropisomers and square-planar/octahedral coordination stereo is absent from the standard layers (IUPAC's organometallic/inorganic extensions are still working-group material, not shipped standard). So Standard InChI is a good equivalence-class key and a bad structure store.

SMILES. OpenSMILES specifies syntax and semantics of reading, but deliberately does NOT resolve the aromaticity model and specifies no canonicalization; "canonical SMILES" is toolkit-proprietary and differs across toolkits and across versions of one toolkit (O'Boyle 2012 measured this on >1.1M ChEMBL/PubChem structures and found, additionally, that Open Babel and InChI disagree on stereocentre perception in ~1.8% of structures). Aromaticity perception divergence is structural, not incidental: Daylight uses an extended Hückel-style model; Open Babel implements a single "close to Daylight" model; RDKit's default model differs by design (fused-ring envelopes considered, only rings ≤24 atoms in fused systems, its own electron-contribution table, exocyclic C=O removing the ring atom's contribution) and RDKit also ships Simple and MDL models (MDL: C/N only, no five-membered independent aromatics); OEChem ships five models and rejects SSSR outright. Concrete divergence: azulene requires the explicit fusion single bond ("c1ccc-2cccc2cc1") to be unambiguous because per-ring vs envelope perception disagree; 2-pyridone-type systems and exocyclic-double-bond rings flip between models; an aromatic-form SMILES that cannot be kekulized under the reader's model (no perfect matching on the delocalization subgraph) is invalid in one toolkit and accepted in another. Consequence: an aromatic-typed bond is not a fact about a molecule; it is a fact about a perception model.

REACTIONS. RInChI 1.00 (software release 2017; an update is maintained under the InChI Trust GitHub umbrella) layers: version, reactants, products, agents (each as sorted InChIs), direction flag d+/d-/d= (equilibrium), and a no-structure counter. It deliberately records NO stoichiometric coefficients, NO atom mapping, NO conditions, and inherits Standard InChI's tautomer collapse and stereo limits. It is therefore an interchange/lookup key, not a reaction semantics carrier. Reaction SMILES carries optional atom-map integers, but map numbers are arbitrary annotations — never canonical; atom-atom mapping in general is NP-hard (subgraph isomorphism), symmetry makes multiple mappings simultaneously valid, and the current best tools are heuristic/ML (RXNMapper 99.4% self-consistent on USPTO but 83.7% on a curated golden set; LocalMapper ~98.5% calibrated). Stage A's flag is confirmed: atom mapping is evidence-grade and partial, never identity.

REGISTRIES. ChEBI: manually curated, CC BY 4.0, monthly data releases (TSV/SDF/OBO/OWL) with nightly ontology-file refreshes after the 2025 re-engineering; entries carry curated structure, and crucially ChEBI already models tautomer/protonation distinctions as ontology relations (conjugate acid/base), which matches the species-vs-tautomer-class split below. PubChem: CIDs are minted by an automated standardization/normalization pipeline over contributor SIDs; data is free of use restrictions including commercial use (NCBI policy; some contributed content may carry third-party copyright, but identifiers and structures are unencumbered in practice). CAS: the REGISTRY is proprietary; CAS's own licensing FAQ requires a license to publish CAS RNs or to back a public "search by CAS RN" feature; CAS Common Chemistry (~500K substances) is CC BY-NC 4.0 — the NC clause is incompatible with an MIT OR Apache-2.0 commercial product vendoring it. CAS RNs can only be pass-through user annotations.

BALANCE CHECKING. Balancing is exact rational linear algebra: species composition vectors (element counts + one charge row) form a matrix; a declared reaction with declared rational coefficients is checked by verifying the coefficient vector lies in the nullspace — this sidesteps the classic failure of "solve for coefficients": when the nullspace has dimension >1 (composite/coupled reactions) there are infinitely many balancings and no canonical one, and when dimension 0 the reaction is impossible. Corner cases with shipped precedent in existing balancers: the free electron e⁻ as a species (empty element vector, charge −1) for half-reactions; ionic charges must be conserved as their own row (mass balance alone passes charge-unbalanced redox); hydrates (CuSO4·5H2O) must parse dot-components into one composition vector with rational multipliers; radicals balance elementally but their identity requires a spin/unpaired-electron field the InChI key cannot carry; polymers, Markush, and nonstoichiometric solids have no finite composition vector at all — they must be typed as balance-unsupported rather than approximated. Isotopes add a subtlety: balance over elements is the chemistry-default; a reaction declared isotopically labeled must balance over nuclides instead, and mixing the two silently is an error.

The through-line for the IR: every identity string in chemistry is a lossy projection of a typed molecular graph under a pinned normalization algorithm. The graph is the semantics; the strings are keys. The fidelity rule ("molecular graphs never encoded as generic predicates") is met by making the graph a first-class typed value, and the fail-closed rule is met by refusing any import whose recomputed key disagrees with its supplied key.

## design_recommendations

- **decision**: Canonical structure representation | **recommendation**: The IR-native typed molecular graph is canonical: atoms typed as (element, optional nuclide, formal charge, unpaired-electron/spin field), explicit bond orders only (Kekulé/localized), explicit hydrogen counts, typed stereo descriptors (tetrahedral, double-bond, allene; extensible enum). No aromatic bond type exists in the IR. Identity keys are computed FROM this graph, never the reverse. | **rationale**: Every string format is lossy (InChI: tautomer collapse, stereo gaps, no spin; SMILES: aromaticity model-dependent, no normative canonical form). Fidelity Gate 1 requires molecular graphs as typed operations, and a graph with localized bonds is the only representation every toolchain can agree on deterministically. | **alternatives_rejected**: Standard InChI string as the stored structure (cannot represent a specific tautomer, radical spin state, or aliphatic-N stereo); canonical SMILES as canonical form (no normative canonicalization exists — toolkit- and version-dependent per O'Boyle 2012); storing aromatic-typed bonds (imports a perception model into identity).
- **decision**: Canonical identity key and pinned algorithm | **recommendation**: Standard InChI + InChIKey, computed by the vendored IUPAC implementation pinned at an exact version (1.07.x line, currently 1.07.5; MIT since 1.07.1 so vendoring is license-clean), is the product's canonical species-CLASS key. Every identity claim records algorithm version + options in provenance; an InChI version bump is a recorded supersession event (A.5.3), never an in-place recompute. | **rationale**: InChI is the only identifier with a single normative implementation and a standards body behind it; MIT relicensing makes static linking into an MIT OR Apache-2.0 product possible. Version pinning is mandatory because normalization behavior changes between versions (1.06→1.07 fixed bugs affecting outputs). | **alternatives_rejected**: InChIKey alone as identity (hash; collisions acknowledged, not invertible, cannot be diffed on disagreement); toolkit canonical SMILES as key (non-interoperable); 'Universal SMILES' (research proposal, not a maintained standard).
- **decision**: Role assignment: canonical / import / presentation | **recommendation**: Canonical: IR molecular graph + Standard InChI class key. Import formats: SMILES (OpenSMILES reading rules), molfile/SDF (V2000/V3000), InChI, ChEBI/PubChem records. Presentation: any SMILES or InChI rendering, plus toolkit canonical SMILES explicitly labeled with the generating toolkit+version — presentation forms are never compared for identity. | **rationale**: Matches the evidence: SMILES and molfile are what users and databases actually have; InChI is what databases key on; nothing downstream may depend on a form whose bytes depend on an unpinned third-party algorithm. | **alternatives_rejected**: Accepting 'canonical SMILES' from users as identity-bearing (silently toolkit-relative); refusing SMILES import entirely (kills adoption; OpenSMILES reading is well-specified even though writing canonically is not).
- **decision**: Normative aromaticity/perception model | **recommendation**: None at the semantic level, by design. Aromatic-form SMILES input is kekulized at import under OpenSMILES matching rules; kekulization failure (no perfect matching) is a fail-closed compile error naming the delocalization subgraph. The IR stores only the kekulized graph. If a workflow needs 'is aromatic', it is a computed predicate explicitly parameterized by a named model (aromatic(ring, model: rdkit_default | daylight | mdl)) — a model-relative claim, never identity. | **rationale**: Daylight vs RDKit vs OpenBabel vs OEChem provably disagree (azulene fusion-bond ambiguity; RDKit's 24-atom fused-ring cap and exocyclic-C=O electron stealing; MDL's C/N-only model). A product that picks one model silently inherits its disagreements; a product that stores Kekulé structures and treats aromaticity as a tagged computation cannot be wrong about it. | **alternatives_rejected**: Adopting the Daylight model as normative (OpenSMILES itself declined to; would misclassify inputs from RDKit/MDL-sourced pipelines); storing whatever the import toolkit perceived (unreproducible identity).
- **decision**: Ambiguous-identity failure contract (the negative corpus case) | **recommendation**: On import, the pipeline always recomputes Standard InChI from the parsed graph with the pinned implementation. Fail-closed triggers: (a) supplied InChI/InChIKey ≠ recomputed; (b) two sources give one registry ID two distinct recomputed InChIs; (c) kekulization or stereo parse failure. Error payload (invalid_input, or 'conflicting' when two attributed sources disagree): both identifier strings, the first differing InChI LAYER (formula/connectivity/H/charge/stereo/isotope), both provenance records, pinned algorithm version+options, and the import-stage step that diverged. The verification corpus derives its ambiguous-identity negative directly from trigger (b). | **rationale**: Layer-granular diffs make disagreement diagnosable (a /t-layer diff means stereo perception, a /c diff means a different molecule) and are cheap because InChI layers are positionally parseable. Recompute-and-compare is the only oracle that does not trust any single importer. | **alternatives_rejected**: Trusting the supplied identifier (defaulting — banned by fail-closed contract); majority vote among toolkits (paraconsistent-flavored resolution Stage A already rejected; resolution only by declared policy per A.5.2).
- **decision**: Type ladder for chemical entities | **recommendation**: Distinct IR types, no subtyping shortcuts: Element (Z) → Nuclide (Z, A) → AtomSite (element, optional nuclide, formal charge, unpaired e⁻) as graph vertices; MolecularGraph (structure); ChemicalSpecies = MolecularGraph fixed in tautomer AND protonation state (micro-species); TautomerClass = the Standard-InChI mobile-H equivalence class, a distinct type linked species↔class; Substance/Mixture (composition over species with rational or measured fractions) and Phase (species/substance + state descriptor) as separate types; MoleculeInstance/amount only inside reaction instances and measurements. | **rationale**: InChI's own architecture proves species and tautomer-class are different objects (standard vs fixed-H layers); ChEBI models protonation pairs as conjugate acid/base relations between distinct entries — the registry the product binds to already keeps these apart. Collapsing any rung reintroduces the acetamide/acetimidic-acid identity bug at the type level. | **alternatives_rejected**: One 'molecule' type with optional annotations (undoes fail-closed: a missing protonation state would have to default); making species identity pH-contextual (see next row).
- **decision**: Species identity is context-free; speciation is a lifted relation | **recommendation**: A ChemicalSpecies is NOT pH-contextual: 'acetate' and 'acetic acid' are two species forever. The pH-dependent fact ('at pH 7 the dominant micro-species of CHEBI:15366-class is acetate') is a speciation CLAIM in a context carrying pH/temperature/ionic strength as typed context variables, resolved by the A.5 explicit-lifting machinery; a query naming a tautomer-class where a micro-species is required, without a context that determines one, is a fail-closed missing-context error. | **rationale**: This reuses the already-decided Cyc-style context/lifting design instead of inventing chemical-identity fuzziness; it makes the biology handoff clean (bio databases mix micro-species and class identifiers notoriously). | **alternatives_rejected**: pH-parameterized identity (identity becomes non-Leibnizian; two contexts would make one species unequal to itself); silently normalizing everything to the major species at pH 7.4 (a default — banned).
- **decision**: Reaction vs ReactionInstance; RInChI's role | **recommendation**: Reaction = typed object: multiset of (ChemicalSpecies, exact rational coefficient) for reactants and products, typed agent list, direction ∈ {forward, backward, equilibrium}. ReactionInstance = a Reaction + conditions (T, P, solvent, catalyst loading — typed context variables) + amounts; kinetic claims (rate law, constants), equilibrium claims (K, standard state), and thermodynamic claims (ΔrG°, ΔrH°) are three distinct claim kinds attached to Reaction/ReactionInstance with kind-mandatory fields per A.5.1, never fields of the Reaction itself. RInChI 1.00 is computed as an export/lookup key only, with its losses (no coefficients, no maps, no conditions, tautomer collapse) documented at the projection function's type. | **rationale**: RInChI's own spec drops stoichiometry and conditions — it cannot be the reaction semantics; but its Web-RInChIKey enables cross-database reaction lookup for free. Splitting equilibrium/kinetics/thermodynamics as claim kinds is forced by the two-axis taxonomy: 'K=1.8e-5 at 25°C' is evidence-bearing and regime-scoped, not structural. | **alternatives_rejected**: RInChI as canonical reaction identity (loses exactly the coefficients the balance checker needs); putting conditions on Reaction (conflates class with instance, breaking evidence attachment); reaction SMILES as canonical (inherits every SMILES ambiguity times two sides).
- **decision**: Atom mapping is typed-partial evidence, never identity | **recommendation**: AtomMap = an optional, provenance-mandatory partial injection reactant-atoms→product-atoms attached to a Reaction as a claim (kind: expert annotation, or imported assertion with tool+version+score). Multiple competing maps coexist in the evidence graph; symmetry-equivalent maps are equal under a declared automorphism quotient. Reaction equality and balance checking never read maps. No map is ever computed silently; a mechanism-dependent query with no map in context fails closed naming the missing map. | **rationale**: Confirmed Stage A flag: mapping is NP-hard, symmetry-degenerate (multiple valid maps for one reaction), and current tools are heuristic ML with 83–98% golden-set accuracy — that is evidence quality, not identifier quality. The typed-partiality ruling covers it exactly. | **alternatives_rejected**: Canonical atom mapping (no sound algorithm exists; even 'correct' is ill-defined under symmetry); mapping as part of reaction identity (two labs' identical reactions would differ); running RXNMapper at import (an LLM-adjacent nondeterministic authoring aid — allowed only authoring-side behind gates per A.5.9, its output entering as an attributed claim).
- **decision**: Registry bindings and the CAS boundary | **recommendation**: Bind: ChEBI (primary; CC BY 4.0; pin the monthly release identifier in every context; entity references are (CHEBI:id, release)); PubChem CID (secondary cross-reference; free without use restriction; record retrieval date since PubChem is continuous, not versioned-released). Do NOT bind or vendor CAS: CAS RNs are accepted only as opaque user-supplied annotation strings with a documented no-verification, no-redistribution-guarantee status; CAS Common Chemistry (CC BY-NC 4.0) must not be vendored into an MIT OR Apache-2.0 distributed product. | **rationale**: Transcription-only governance requires a mechanizable oracle: ChEBI releases are versioned artifacts (monthly, in OBO/OWL/SDF) an importer can pin and diff; PubChem standardization is documented and data unrestricted. CAS licensing explicitly requires a license to publish RNs or back public RN search, and the NC clause on Common Chemistry contaminates commercial downstream use. | **alternatives_rejected**: CAS as a registry (proprietary; the product's license promise would be false); Common Chemistry vendored 'because it's open' (BY-NC ≠ open for a commercial product); unpinned 'latest ChEBI' (breaks context reproducibility per A.5.3).
- **decision**: Balance checking: exact rational verification with typed corner cases | **recommendation**: Composition vectors over ℚ: one row per element (or per nuclide when the reaction is declared isotope-tracking — a per-reaction typed mode, never mixed) plus one charge row. The checker VERIFIES the declared coefficient vector is in the nullspace (mass and charge simultaneously); it never solves for coefficients in the compile gate. First-class corner types: e⁻ as a species (empty element vector, charge −1) so half-reactions balance; radicals balance normally but carry the spin field (their InChI class key is documented as spin-blind); hydrates/solvates parse dot-components into one rational composition. Polymers, Markush, and nonstoichiometric species get no finite composition vector: a reaction containing one is representable but its balance check returns the 'unsupported' disposition naming the offending species — typed partiality, never a silent pass. Unbalanced ⇒ invalid_model with the per-row (element/charge) imbalance as payload, per A.4. | **rationale**: Verification dodges the two mathematical traps of solving: nullspace dimension >1 (composite reactions have infinitely many balancings — no canonical answer exists to default to) and dimension 0. Charge as a mandatory extra row is what catches charge-unbalanced redox that mass balance passes. Existing balancers demonstrate e⁻/hydrate/charge handling is tractable. | **alternatives_rejected**: Auto-balancing user reactions (dim>1 forces an arbitrary choice = a default; banned); integer-only coefficients (rational coefficients are standard in thermochemistry, e.g. ½O₂); skipping charge balance (standard failure mode); treating polymers via average formula (a convention enforced by documentation — an explicit Gate 1 failure).

## adopt

InChI software 1.07.x (pin exact patch, currently 1.07.5; MIT license since 1.07.1) vendored as the single identity oracle, version+options recorded in every identity claim. Standard InChI (1S) as species-CLASS key; the /f fixed-H mechanism's distinction adopted as the type split species-vs-tautomer-class. OpenSMILES reading semantics for SMILES import (kekulization mandatory at ingress). RInChI 1.00 + Web-RInChIKey as reaction export/lookup keys only. ChEBI monthly releases (CC BY 4.0) pinned per context; PubChem CID as unrestricted cross-reference with retrieval date. Exact rational linear algebra (ℚ nullspace membership) for mass+charge balance, with e⁻-as-species and dot-disconnected hydrates. GUM-typed measurement claims (already Stage A) for spectra/measured properties attached to species/substances as evidence with provenance — never identity-bearing.

## avoid

Canonical SMILES as identity (toolkit- and version-relative; breaks interchange — O'Boyle measured cross-toolkit divergence and ~1.8% OB-vs-InChI stereo disagreement). Any single normative aromaticity model stored in identity (Daylight/RDKit/MDL/OEChem provably disagree; azulene et al. flip) — causes silent cross-source identity splits. Trusting supplied identifiers without recompute (defaulting; the ambiguous-identity case becomes undetectable). Treating InChI as a structure store (tautomer collapse, spin-blindness, aliphatic-N and coordination stereo gaps become silent data loss). Canonical or silently computed atom mapping (NP-hard, symmetry-degenerate, ML tools 83–98% — presenting it as fact fabricates mechanism evidence). CAS REGISTRY or CAS Common Chemistry as bound registries (proprietary / CC BY-NC 4.0 — poisons the MIT OR Apache-2.0 + commercial-hosted plan). Auto-balancing (nullspace dim>1 has no canonical solution; auto-choice is a hidden default). Mixing element-level and nuclide-level balance in one reaction without a declared mode (labeled-compound bookkeeping silently corrupts). Unpinned 'latest' registry data (breaks A.5.3 context pinning).
