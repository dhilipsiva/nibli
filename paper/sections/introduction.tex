\section{Introduction}

A technical decision can be logically justified and still be difficult to audit.
Its premises may have changed, a negative result may depend on a closed-world
assumption, and the returned explanation may describe a different execution
profile from the one that produced the verdict. These problems become concrete
when an analyzer supplies findings and a separate policy layer decides which
ones permit a release. Removing a waiver should affect the decision and its
evidence in a predictable way; an unfinished search should not masquerade as
proof that no problem exists.

Nibli\footnote{Source repository: \url{https://github.com/dhilipsiva/nibli}.
Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} identifies the evaluated baseline and experimental
source fingerprint.} is an implemented symbolic reasoning engine with a formal predicate-call
surface, typed generated witnesses, bounded demand-driven evaluation, selective
finite materialization, retained premise identities, and optional proof
envelopes. It exposes operationally distinct definitive, unresolved, and
resource-limited outcomes through native and WebAssembly execution paths.
The system's purpose is to make the relationship between accepted premises,
evaluation choices, and evidence inspectable.

These ingredients have substantial precedents. Controlled languages connect
restricted syntax to logic~\cite{ace}; tabled logic programming manages reusable
subgoals and completion~\cite{xsb}; Datalog systems provide high-performance
evaluation and provenance~\cite{souffle,souffle-provenance}. Formal models coupled
to differential implementation tests are also an established verification
approach~\cite{cedar}. We therefore do not propose a new general inference
calculus or claim that auditability is unique to Nibli.

The contribution is a systems account and reproducible evaluation of their
particular integration. First, we state the semantic and operational contracts,
including the limits of closed-world answers, withdrawal, generated identity,
and trusted computation. Second, we distinguish the Lean proof results from
their implementation correspondence and from the weaker structural envelope
check. Third, focused new experiments measure policy joins, recursive
reachability, depth and materialization tradeoffs, evidence cost, premise
transitions, and portable behavior. The protocol retains unfavorable workloads,
non-definitive answers, and process cutoffs.

The research questions concern consistency under premise change, performance
and completeness under evaluation choices, and behavior across execution
surfaces. The resulting artifact includes the exact workloads, raw observations,
proof envelopes, analysis scripts, and dependency locks. It supports a bounded
claim about the evaluated engine and its evidence rather than a claim that
symbolic reasoning certifies input truth or makes every logical problem tractable.
