\section{Limitations and threats to validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

The evaluation is intentionally local and finite. Synthetic policy records
exercise a selected join pattern; a directed chain exercises one particularly
unfavorable recursive rule. They do not represent the distribution of real
authorization policies or static analyses. Larger, denser graphs, different
selectivities, many rule alternatives, mixed query batches, and adversarial
update schedules can produce different costs. The tested fact counts are input
surface facts, not internal event-role tuples or full derived closures.
No general scalability claim follows from their absolute latency.
In particular, a timeout while loading through repeated single-statement API
calls does not establish that every possible ingestion strategy fails at that
dataset size. It does establish a cost of the measured public-API workflow.

Implementation order is randomized, but the experiment does not isolate the
machine from its host operating system or control CPU frequency and competing
workstation activity. Warm-ups reduce first-use effects without making the
measurements warm in-process service timings. Peak RSS includes runtime and
library overhead and cannot be read as the marginal size of a knowledge base.
The 30-second cutoff also means that medians among completed samples can be
optimistic for a partially censored cell. The outcome table, raw samples, and
coverage plots preserve the information needed to notice that bias.

Cross-system equivalence is restricted to the declared finite fragment.
The renderers are independent translations from common data, but they share
that data generator and can share a misunderstanding of its intended semantics.
Hand-derived discriminators and the existing independent solver gates reduce
this risk without eliminating it. Nibli demand-driven queries and baseline
materialization perform different amounts of internal work. The result is a
comparison of delivering the requested answers, not an implementation-independent
measure of inference steps. clingo also supports richer multi-shot workflows
than the one-process invocation used here~\cite{clingo}; this experiment does
not evaluate them.

Formal assurance remains partial. The Lean models do not prove compiler
correctness, transaction implementation, all quantifier and modal behavior,
materialization projection, or the complete WebAssembly toolchain.
The trace theorem has explicit semantic hypotheses, and most correspondence
tests are sampled. The source audit checks whether citations identify current
premises, not whether each inference follows from them. Envelopes are not
authenticated attestations. These limits preclude describing the system as a
fully independently verified prover on every accepted input.

Closed-world policy quality is outside the experiment. A missing analyzer
record, incorrect waiver, or incorrectly formalized policy can change an answer
without exposing an engine defect. The example chooses positive authorization
evidence, but an application must establish when its case records are complete
enough to support a negative decision. Generated witnesses are logical
representatives, not discovered real-world entities; separate witnesses do not
by themselves prove that the represented individuals are unequal.

Finally, cross-runtime agreement covers the retained scenarios and supported
profiles. It does not promise identical memory usage, latency, resource limits,
or proof byte order. A synchronous external compute call is a host-side
operation and is not automatically bounded by guest instruction fuel.
External computation is excluded from the comparative workloads, and neither
its correctness nor its real-world freshness is certified by this study.
